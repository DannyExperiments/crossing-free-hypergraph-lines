## Exact question

- Alexander Dobler, *Algorithmic Aspects of Ordering Problems in Information Visualization*, TU Wien thesis (2026), §5.1, printed pp. 66–67, defines the representation. The first question of §5.6, printed p. 85 (PDF p. 101), asks its recognition complexity. [Official record](https://doi.org/10.34726/hss.2026.139471) · [Thesis PDF](https://repositum.tuwien.at/bitstream/20.500.12708/226672/1/Dobler%20Alexander%20-%202026%20-%20Algorithmic%20Aspects%20of%20Ordering%20Problems%20in...pdf#page=101)
- Dobler, Kobourov, Mondal and Nöllenburg, *Representing Hypergraphs by Point-Line Incidences*, DMTCS 28:3 (2026), §§3 and 7, gives the same definition and first open question. Distinct vertices map to distinct plane points, distinct edges to distinct complete affine lines and point-line incidence is exact. Geometric intersections are allowed precisely at a common hypergraph vertex. Disjoint hyperedges require parallel lines. Intersecting hyperedges can share a multiple point. Strictness follows for complete distinct lines. The pinned catalog Q233 states these rules explicitly. ## Existing results and overlap

- Eun Jung Kim, Arnaud de Mesmay and Tillmann Miltzow, *Representing Matroids over the Reals is ∃ℝ-complete*, DMTCS 26:2 (2024), **Theorem 1** in the journal PDF, proves real representability hard for rank-three matroids given by fixed-rank combinatorial data. Its first proof uses an arithmetic reduction, and Theorem 2 supplies a second route from order-type realizability. The recovered Q233 proof translates their theorem for **unoriented** matroids. [Journal article](https://doi.org/10.46298/dmtcs.10810) · [Theorem PDF](https://dmtcs.episciences.org/articles/13924/download#page=5)
- Lukas Kühne, Tomasz Szemberg and Halszka Tutaj-Gasińska, *Line arrangements with many triple points*, §2.1, explicitly identifies realizability of a simple matroid over a field with realizing its lattice of flats as the intersection lattice of a hyperplane arrangement. In rank three each rank-two flat yields a projective line intersection. The translation to Q233 records **all** rank-two flats, including the ones with just two elements. A line at infinity avoiding the finitely many crossings gives the affine chart. A reverse map reads each dependent triple from concurrency. This is the mathematical reason the known theorem already settles Q233 through a short translation. [Arrangement correspondence](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12215-024-01047-x)
- Dobler et al., §4.1 Theorem 2, proves ordinary line recognition hard on rank-three linear hypergraphs using Pappus gadgets to force collinearity and noncollinearity. Corollary 1 then applies point-line duality for ordinary line recognition with maximum vertex degree three. Both address a model allowing unmarked line intersections. Section 5.1 Theorems 7 and 8 give polynomial-time crossing-free line algorithms when hyperedge size is at most two or maximum vertex degree is at most two. Q233 output has no claimed constant rank or degree bound. Section 5.2 concerns segments and the related catalog Q234 is outside scope. [Theorem 2 and Corollary 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.13985v4#S4.SS1) · [Crossing-free special cases](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.13985v4#S5.SS1)
- Simple pseudoline stretchability preserves the order of crossings. Bare point-line incidence discards that order, and in a simple arrangement every pair crosses at its own vertex. A direct forgetful encoding of such a stretchability input erases the distinguishing data. The cited matroid result avoids this issue by allowing prescribed multiple intersections.

## Scope and remaining uncertainty

The recovered theorem is ∃ℝ-completeness even for linear hypergraphs with pairwise intersecting hyperedges. The source rank is the dimension of the representing vector matroid. A hypergraph's rank is its largest hyperedge size, a different parameter. The check used the official thesis, current journal and arXiv versions, the pinned catalog, the journal matroid theorem and the arrangement correspondence. It also queried exact hypergraph terminology and arrangement-intersection-lattice recognition. The bounded check cannot establish first publication. The optional rationality appendix is outside this candidate's recognition claim.
